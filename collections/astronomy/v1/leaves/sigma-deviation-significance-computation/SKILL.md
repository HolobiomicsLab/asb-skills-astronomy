# sigma-deviation-significance-computation

## Summary

Computes the uncertainty-weighted (sigma) deviation between a newly derived planetary radius and a previously published value, to test whether two photometric determinations of the same planet are statistically consistent. Applied here to compare the CHEOPS+TESS-derived radius of HIP 29442 d (1.538 ± 0.049 R⊕) against the median radius from the TESS-only discovery paper.

## When to use

Use when a refit of transit photometry (e.g. joint CHEOPS+TESS modelling) yields a planetary radius with an uncertainty, and you need to quantify its inconsistency with a discovery-paper value in units of sigma against a threshold such as 3σ.

## When NOT to use

- The comparison value lacks a published uncertainty, so an uncertainty-weighted sigma deviation cannot be formed and only a raw difference could be reported
- The planet's transit has high per-transit SNR and dense observational coverage (no large data gaps), where the paper's caution about TESS-only radii for small planets does not apply
- You need radius posteriors themselves rather than a comparison against a literature value — use the full plaNETic internal-structure inference instead of this significance test

## Inputs

- numpy .npy posterior sample file for the planet (e.g. xxx_posterior.npy in the 'posteriors' subfolder)
- Median radius and uncertainty reported in the TESS-only discovery paper for planet d
- 3σ significance threshold

## Outputs

- Derived radius and uncertainty (e.g. 1.538 ± 0.049 R⊕ for planet d)
- Deviation in units of sigma between the derived radius and the discovery-paper median
- sigma_deviation_planet_d.csv recording the deviation value and the threshold comparison
- Binary consistency verdict against the 3σ threshold

## How to apply

Load the posterior samples for the planet from the 'xxx_posterior.npy' file in the 'posteriors' subfolder (generated by running the system's 'run_grid_xxx.py' executable in plaNETic). Summarize the posterior to recover the derived radius and its uncertainty — for HIP 29442 d this is 1.538 ± 0.049 R⊕ from joint CHEOPS (9.6 days) and two TESS sectors modelling. Retrieve the median radius from the TESS-only discovery paper, then compute the uncertainty-weighted difference (|r_new − r_median| / σ_combined, here using the new radius's uncertainty) in Python. Compare the resulting deviation against the 3σ significance threshold; a deviation exceeding 3σ flags the two determinations as statistically inconsistent. For HIP 29442 d this comparison reproduces the paper's conclusion that caution is required when using TESS photometry for small planets with low per-transit SNR and large gaps between observations, and the result is written to sigma_deviation_planet_d.csv.

## Related tools

- **plaNETic** (Generates the xxx_posterior.npy radius posterior samples that the deviation is computed from) — https://github.com/joannegger/plaNETic
- **Python** (Python 3.9 environment with numpy used to load, summarize, and compare the posterior samples)
- **CHEOPS** (Provides 9.6 days of photometry jointly modelled with TESS to produce the refined radius used in the comparison)
- **TESS** (Two sectors of photometry jointly modelled with CHEOPS; the TESS-only discovery radii serve as the comparison values)

## Examples

```
import numpy as np; samples = np.load('posteriors/xxx_posterior.npy'); r_new, sigma = 1.538, 0.049; r_med = 1.559; dev = abs(r_new - r_med)/sigma
```

- The threshold comparison in sigma_deviation_planet_d.csv flags the deviation as significant (>3σ), consistent with the paper's caution conclusion
- Posterior file has the expected numpy array structure produced by plaNETic's run_grid executable (posteriors stored in a 'posteriors' subfolder)

## Limitations

- The caution applies specifically to small planets with low per-transit SNR and large gaps between observations; planets b and c do not show this >3σ behaviour
- plaNETic needs access to a GPU to run efficiently; CPU-only runs drastically increase computation time
- If plaNETic cannot find the trained DNNs, this is an issue with GitHub's Large File Storage, requiring contact with the authors
- The deviation test depends on the discovery paper reporting a median radius with a quoted uncertainty; only the uncertainty-weighted comparison is meaningful
